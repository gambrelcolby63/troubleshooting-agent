# Electrical Safety Before Hands-On Work (NFPA 70E concepts)
<!-- Self-written plain-language summary of general electrical-safety concepts. Not a substitute for the standard, your site procedures, or a qualified person. -->

## 1. Establish an electrically safe work condition (ESWC)
The default is to de-energize equipment before working on it. The general sequence is: identify every possible source of supply (including backfeeds, generators, UPS outputs, capacitors and CT/VT secondaries); open the disconnecting devices; visually verify the blades or breaker are open or racked out where possible; apply lockout/tagout (LOTO) devices; release or block stored energy (springs, capacitors, batteries); test for absence of voltage with a properly rated tester; and apply temporary protective grounds where induced voltage or re-energization is possible. Only after these steps is the equipment considered electrically safe.

## 2. Test before touch: verify absence of voltage
Use a meter or tester rated for the voltage and category (CAT rating) of the circuit. Verify the tester works on a known live source, test every phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground combination on the de-energized equipment, then re-verify the tester on the known live source (live-dead-live). Wear the PPE required for the shock and arc-flash risk while testing, because until absence of voltage is proven the equipment must be treated as energized.

## 3. Lockout/tagout (LOTO)
Each worker applies a personal lock and tag to each energy-isolating device. A tag alone is not a lock. Locks are removed only by the person who applied them, following site procedure. Before re-energizing, confirm tools are removed, guards and covers are reinstalled, grounds are removed, and all personnel are clear.

## 4. Risk assessment and PPE
Before any task inside the limited approach boundary or arc-flash boundary, perform a shock risk assessment and an arc-flash risk assessment. Select PPE (arc-rated clothing, face shield or hood, voltage-rated gloves with leather protectors, safety glasses, hearing protection) based on the equipment label or the site's arc-flash study. If the incident energy or PPE category is unknown, stop and get the information from a qualified person; do not guess.

## 5. Energized work is the exception
Working on or near exposed energized parts is only justified when de-energizing creates a greater hazard or is infeasible (for example some diagnostic voltage measurements). It requires a qualified person, a documented justification and energized electrical work permit per site policy, a job briefing, and the correct PPE. Convenience, schedule pressure, or "it will only take a minute" are NOT valid reasons to work energized.

## 6. Never defeat interlocks or safety devices
Do not bypass, jumper, or defeat door interlocks, ground-fault protection, protective relays, trip circuits, or grounding conductors to get equipment running or to save time. A tripping device is giving information; find the cause. Lifting a ground or jumpering a trip contact can expose people to lethal shock or arc-flash and can destroy equipment.
