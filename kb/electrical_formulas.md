# Basic Electrical Formulas for Troubleshooting
<!-- Self-written. Standard textbook relationships. -->

## 1. Ohm's law
Voltage = current × resistance (V = I × R). Current = V / R. Resistance = V / I. Example: a coil that draws 0.5 A from 24 V has an effective resistance of 48 ohms (for AC coils the impedance includes inductive reactance, so a DC resistance reading will be lower).

## 2. Power
Single-phase or DC power: P = V × I (watts), and for AC with power factor P = V × I × pf. Three-phase real power: P = √3 × V(line-to-line) × I(line) × pf. Apparent power in VA or kVA leaves out the power factor. Divide watts by 1000 for kW.

## 3. Ratios and percent error
CT or VT ratio: primary = secondary × (rated primary / rated secondary). Percent error = (measured − expected) / expected × 100. Polarization index = 10-minute insulation resistance / 1-minute insulation resistance.

## 4. Voltage drop and unbalance
Voltage drop along a conductor = current × conductor resistance (both ways for a two-wire circuit). Percent voltage unbalance ≈ maximum deviation from the average phase voltage / average phase voltage × 100. Even a few percent of voltage unbalance causes a much larger current unbalance and heating in motors.
