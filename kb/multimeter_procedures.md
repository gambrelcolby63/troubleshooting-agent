# Multimeter and Clamp Meter Procedures
<!-- Self-written. General practice only. -->

## 1. Choosing and checking the meter
Use a meter whose CAT rating (CAT III / CAT IV) and voltage rating meet or exceed the circuit. Inspect leads for cracked insulation and use fused current inputs. Check the meter on a known source before and after use (live-dead-live).

## 2. Measuring voltage
Set the meter to AC or DC volts as appropriate, connect the common lead first and remove it last. Measure phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground on three-phase systems. A large difference between phases (voltage unbalance) or a missing phase points to a blown fuse, open conductor, or failed supply. Voltage measurements on energized equipment require the PPE and qualification described in the safety document.

## 3. Measuring current with a clamp meter
Clamp around a single conductor, not the whole cable (the fields cancel). Compare each phase to the motor or load nameplate full-load amps (FLA). Unequal phase currents suggest single phasing, unbalanced supply voltage, or a winding problem. Never break into a circuit to insert a meter in series when a clamp meter will do.

## 4. Resistance and continuity: de-energized only
Resistance, continuity and diode tests must only be done on de-energized, locked-out circuits with capacitors discharged. Use continuity tests to find open fuses, open coils, broken wires and failed contacts. A coil reading open (OL) is failed; a reading near zero ohms may indicate a shorted coil. Compare with the nameplate or a known good part.

## 5. Interpreting readings
Voltage present across an open contact or fuse in a live circuit means that device is open (the voltage drop appears across the open point). Zero volts across a closed contact or good fuse is normal. When chasing a control circuit fault, move the meter point-by-point from the source toward the load until the voltage disappears; the open is between the last good point and the first bad point.
